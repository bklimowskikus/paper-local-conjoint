# Reviewer 2: Main Issues Before Submission

The manuscript fits *Electoral Studies* and has a strong randomized design, but several issues weaken its current research-note contribution.

## Major issues

1. **Ineligible councillor profile.** The outsider condition describes a candidate living in another municipality. Such a candidate appears legally ineligible to run for that municipal council in Poland. This compromises the councillor condition, the office comparison, and part of the pooled municipal-roots estimate. The paper should disclose this limitation, report sensitivity analyses excluding councillor profiles, and treat the office comparison as secondary.

2. **The null office result does not establish invariance.** The omnibus interaction and pairwise marginal-mean comparisons only show that office differences were not detected. The paper should report direct office-by-office differences in the resident-versus-outsider bonuses, with confidence intervals and a substantively justified equivalence range or minimum detectable difference. Claims should be limited to the absence of the predicted office ordering.

3. **The national-alignment estimate is a high-salience endpoint comparison.** The 28--33-point estimates exclude neutral profiles and middle-position respondents and use an explicit cue naming Donald Tusk's government and coalition. They should not be interpreted as a general party-label effect or as evidence of complete nationalization. Separate estimates for pro- and anti-government respondents and an analysis using the full attitude scale would show whether pooling conceals asymmetry.

4. **The theoretical contribution remains narrow.** The stable result is that graded municipal connections matter alongside national political congruence. The interaction is not sufficiently stable to support reinforcement, and the design does not identify why voters value local roots. The paper should frame the office comparison as exploratory and center the research note on coexistence under a strong national cue.

## Overall assessment

The paper is promising but needs substantial reframing before submission. Its strongest version is a concise research note about the persistence of graded municipal-root advantages under national political congruence, with the office findings presented cautiously as secondary evidence.
